The dashed bar is v11 — never suite-tested, drawn as a gap, never interpolated. That single rule is what makes the rest of the page trustworthy.
Spec 08 — where I think you're right
The three honesty rules are the best things in the pack: gaps for untested versions (req 7), discontinuity at definition changes (req 6), and promotability as a literal state rather than something the owner has to infer (req 3). Also right that "nothing needs you" is a designed state, not an empty one — most dashboards can't say that sentence.
…and four places I'd push
1. Two personas, one ranked list, different questions. The owner asks "can I trust it" — a ranking answers that. The returning builder asks "what changed while I was gone" — that's a diff, and no ordering of req 10 produces it. I'd add a "since you were last here" line rather than pretend one list serves both.
2. The discontinuity rule may shred the charts. Evaluators get edited constantly in the first weeks — apply req 6 strictly and early-life trends become confetti. I'd default to "points comparable to today's definitions" and put full history behind a toggle. Same honesty, readable.
3. Plain-language-first everywhere is too blunt. Pay the wordiness tax on the four tiles and the attention list, where decisions happen. On the trends, the person reading axis labels has already opted into detail.
4. A missing answer. "Quality dipped at v15" — the page routes to the SuiteRun, but the real answer is what changed in v15. Version lineage (createdFrom) should hang off every trend point, or the owner's obvious follow-up dead-ends.
What the storyboard settles
Overview is not a Run Detail tab (your question 1) — frames 04 and 13 are different jobs at different altitudes. It's a landing surface, and on open question 1 in your spec: builder lands on canvas, owner lands here, because frames 01–12 all start from the canvas and forcing them through a dashboard would tax the inner loop every time.
Run history (your question 7) belongs in two places for two reasons: the canvas scrubber for "show me the last run instead" during stages 1–2, and a left rail in Run Detail for "compare these two runs" during stage 2. Frame 04's tab bar is where I'd hang it.
Compare (question 5) only appears at frame 11 — it's a stage-4 tool. Putting it in the Run Detail header, as my earlier sketch did, offers it two stages early with nothing to compare against.
