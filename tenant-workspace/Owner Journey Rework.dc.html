11d

Teams instead of projects?

Open question 6. Three ways to answer it, cheapest first.

D1 · No container. Access lives on the product. SIMPLEST
Use case → people, use case → products, product → who has access. No projects, no teams. Every ask you have described fits this model. Cost: the "6 people, 4 products" grid gets wide once there are many of each, and there is nowhere to express "the fraud squad" as a reusable idea.
D2 · Teams as saved people-groups, opt-in. RECOMMENDED
Start at D1. In the access picker, "Save these 4 as a team" appears once a subset is chosen. A team is a named list of use-case people — nothing more: no hosting, no products, no approval of its own. Next install offers Fraud squad (4) as one row. Teams only exist for owners who needed them.
D3 · Teams as real subtenants (today's projects, renamed). HEAVIEST
A team owns its own hosting, products, and access. This is what the prototype has now under the name "project". It matches the member journey (request access to a project) but forces the owner through a container before they can install anything — the friction you just flagged.
The collision to settle first: the member journey requests access to a project. If owners stop creating projects, members need something else to request — the use case itself, or a product within it. D2 answers this as "request access to the use case, then the owner grants products"; D3 keeps projects and the collision goes away but the owner flow stays heavy.
